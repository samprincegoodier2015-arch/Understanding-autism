Different ways of thinking, communicating and experiencing the world are valid.

A reminder: autism is a spectrum, and every autistic person is different. These are possibilities, not rules. Facial expressions cannot tell you exactly what someone feels—when appropriate, ask kindly.

What is autism?

Autism is a lifelong neurodevelopmental difference. It can affect communication, social interaction, sensory experiences, routines and how someone processes information.

💬

Communication

Some people prefer direct language, extra time, writing, pictures or alternative ways to communicate.

🔊

Sensory experiences

Sounds, lights, smells, textures or busy places may feel intense. Sensory needs vary by person and situation.

🔁

Routine and interests

Predictability, repeated movements or favourite interests may bring comfort, joy, regulation or focus.

🧠

Processing time

A pause may mean someone is thinking, processing language or finding words—not that they are ignoring you.

🌦️

Overwhelm

Too much demand or sensory input can become overwhelming. Quiet, space and fewer questions may help.

✨

Strengths and joy

Autistic people may have deep knowledge, creativity, honesty, attention to detail or fresh perspectives. Everyone is different.

Communicate clearly and kindly

Make conversations easier by reducing guesswork. Ask the person what works for them.

Helpful approaches

  • Use clear, direct language.
  • Ask one question at a time and allow time to answer.
  • Explain changes to plans early.
  • Offer speaking, texting, writing or pointing as options.
  • Respect different eye contact, movement and expressions.
  • Check understanding without embarrassing anyone.

Phrases you can use

“Take your time. There's no rush.”
“Would you like me to explain that another way?”
“Would you prefer somewhere quieter?”
“What would make this easier for you?”
